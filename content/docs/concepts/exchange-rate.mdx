title: The exchange rate
description: The monotonic, maturity-locked rate that denominates every mint and redemption.

---

The exchange rate is the spine of every mint and redeem calculation in a market.

It is defined as **assets per `1e7` vault shares**, 1e7-scaled, and read from the
vault's `convert_to_assets`. The yield manager stores it and applies one policy
on top of the raw vault value.

## The rule

> It starts at the vault's rate when the market is created, only ever moves **up**
> while the market is live, and **freezes at maturity**.

**Before maturity.** Each operation refreshes the stored rate to the vault's
current rate, but never lets it fall below the stored value — every update takes
`max(stored, vault_rate)`. So it climbs with the vault's yield and ignores any
dips.

**At maturity.** The rate locks at whatever value it last reached and never
changes again.

## Why it matters

This "only goes up, then freezes" behaviour is exactly what makes PT a fixed
claim and YT a claim on appreciation:

- **PT** redeems after maturity at `max(live_vault_rate, locked_rate)`. The lock
  is a **floor on payout, not a cap** — it stops a late vault-rate dip from
  underpaying face value, and any post-maturity appreciation stays in the yield
  manager as protocol surplus rather than inflating PT.
- **YT** captures precisely the appreciation between deposit and maturity.
  Nothing accrues to it afterwards.

## Rounding

All conversions **floor**, and always in the protocol's favour. Mint amounts,
share returns and accrued-yield payouts all round down, leaving dust in the yield
manager rather than overpaying a user.

<Callout type="warn">
The exchange rate is only as honest as the vault reporting it. YBC does not try
to prove a vault is truthful on-chain — it confines the blast radius to that one
market instead. See [Risks](/concepts/risks).
</Callout>
