A **market** is one instance of the protocol, uniquely identified by the pair
`(vault, maturity)`.

Every market is fully self-contained. It deploys its own yield manager, PT, YT
and AMM pool, and those contracts touch only that market's vault. Markets never
share state, so **one market can never affect another**.

Different maturities on the same vault coexist as entirely independent markets.
The same vault with a March and a June maturity gives you two separate PTs, two
separate YTs and two separate pools.

## Creating a market

Market creation is **permissionless** — anyone may call `create_market` on the
factory with a vault, a maturity, and the curve parameters (`current_apy`,
`apy_min`, `apy_max`, `fee_apy`) that shape the pool's pricing.

The factory then:

<Steps>
<Step>
Validates the inputs. Maturity must be strictly in the future and within
`MAX_MATURITY_HORIZON` (10 years); the AMM constructor bounds the APY band and
the fee.
</Step>
<Step>
Refuses a duplicate `(vault, maturity)`. At most one market exists per pair.
</Step>
<Step>
Deploys the **yield manager**, whose constructor seeds the initial exchange rate
from the vault.
</Step>
<Step>
Deploys **PT** and **YT**, both owned by the yield manager.
</Step>
<Step>
Deploys the **AMM pool** with the APY-derived curve parameters, and registers it
with the yield manager as the sole trusted flash-swap driver.
</Step>
<Step>
Records the market and emits `MarketCreated`, including the creator address so
off-chain curation can decide which markets to surface.
</Step>
</Steps>

## Isolation is the safety model

Because creation is open, a market can be created around a vault that is broken
or outright malicious. That is contained by design: a malicious vault can only
harm the users who opted into *its* market.

<Callout type="warn">
"Permissionless" does not mean "vetted". Which markets appear in a user interface
is an off-chain curation decision. See [Vault integration](/protocol/vault-integration)
for what the protocol assumes about a vault but cannot verify.
</Callout>

## Markets are immutable

`create_market` stamps the factory's current Wasm hashes into the market's yield
manager, PT, YT and pool. None of those four expose an upgrade entrypoint, so a
market can **never** adopt a later version of the code — including its fee
configuration and treasury address, which have no setters.

Only the two singletons can be replaced: the factory is upgradeable, and the
router is stateless enough that a new one works against existing markets
untouched.

The trade-off is deliberate. There is no pause switch and no admin lever over a
live market; in exchange, what you read at entry is what the market will do
forever. Shipping a contract-level fix means creating new markets.
