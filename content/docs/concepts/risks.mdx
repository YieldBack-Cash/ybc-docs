Read this before depositing. None of it is a defect — each item is a deliberate
design trade-off — but each one changes what can happen to your position.

## The vault is trusted on faith

Every market is built on a vault you deposit into, and **YBC does not verify that
vault is honest**. The [exchange rate](/concepts/exchange-rate) that prices every
mint and redemption comes from the vault's own `convert_to_assets`, and PT face
value is derived from it.

A vault that loses share value, or charges a fee on the way out, leaves PT
over-valued against backing that cannot cover it. That is a slow solvency drift
rather than an error, and **nothing on-chain detects it**.

What YBC does instead is confine the damage: a market's contracts touch only its
own vault, so a bad vault can only harm the people who opted into *that* market.

## A market existing is not a market being vetted

[Market creation is permissionless](/concepts/markets). Anyone can spin one up
around any vault, including a broken or malicious one, and the protocol will
deploy it.

Most bad vaults fail loudly — the transaction reverts and you keep your position.
The dangerous case is the one above, which fails quietly. Deciding which markets
are safe to surface is an **off-chain curation judgement**, not something the
contracts enforce. See [Vault integration](/protocol/vault-integration) for how
the protocol actually uses a vault, and which of its guarantees are assumed
rather than checked.

## There is no pause switch

No admin can halt a live market. This cuts both ways:

- **Nobody can freeze your funds.** There is no privileged party who can stop you
  redeeming, and no key whose compromise locks you out.
- **Nobody can rescue them either.** If a market turns out to be built on a bad
  vault, there is no emergency stop and no on-chain intervention. Recovery is a
  curation decision — stop surfacing the market — not a fix.

## Markets are immutable

`create_market` stamps the code into a market permanently. Its yield manager, PT,
YT and pool expose no upgrade entrypoint, and its fee configuration and treasury
address have **no setters at all**.

So what you read at entry is what the market will do forever. A bug found later
cannot be patched in place — it requires creating new markets and migrating.

## Late YT claims pay locked value, not more shares

<Callout type="warn">
After maturity, a yield claim pays the number of shares that its locked-rate
**asset value** buys at the **live** rate. If the vault kept growing, that is
fewer shares than the frozen accrual showed.
</Callout>

Two claims of the same accrual made at different times receive **different share
counts but the same asset value**. This is the intended "no new yield after
maturity" behaviour, not a loss — but it surprises people who compare a share
figure from before maturity against what they actually received.

## What the protocol can never take

For completeness, the limits on privilege:

| Party | Can | Cannot |
|-------|-----|--------|
| Factory admin | Set the code and fee config used by **future** markets; upgrade the factory | Touch any already-deployed market — its setters are one-shot and already spent |
| Treasury admin | Withdraw collected protocol fees; upgrade the treasury | Reach user funds. The treasury only ever receives the reserve cut of trading fees and post-maturity surplus, neither of which belongs to a user |
| Market creator | Create a market around any vault | Nothing security-critical. The address is recorded only for off-chain curation |

## Slippage

Every user-facing swap takes an explicit bound and reverts if it is not met, so a
sandwich attempt fails rather than executing at a bad price. For
[zaps](/using-ybc/zaps) the bound is expressed once in base-asset terms and covers
pool price and vault rate together.
