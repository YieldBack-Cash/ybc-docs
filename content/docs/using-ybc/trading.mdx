Every market has its own AMM pool. The pool trades **PT against V** on a
implied-rate curve; YT is synthesised on demand rather than held as a reserve.

The user-facing operations go through the **router**, a single stable entrypoint
that resolves the right market through the factory and forwards the call. The
router never custodies funds.

## Trading PT

PT trades directly against the pool's two reserves:

| Direction | Router call |
|-----------|-------------|
| Buy PT with vault shares | `swap_v_for_pt` |
| Sell PT for vault shares | `swap_pt_for_v` |

Buying PT below face value and holding to maturity is the fixed-rate trade — the
discount at purchase *is* your locked-in rate.

## Trading YT

The pool holds no YT. Buying and selling YT is a **flash swap**: a PT borrow from
the pool paired with a mint or burn in the yield manager, all inside one call.

| Direction | Router call |
|-----------|-------------|
| Buy YT with vault shares | `swap_v_for_yt` |
| Sell YT for vault shares | `swap_yt_for_v` |

From your side it behaves like an ordinary swap. You pay the YT price and
receive YT. The pool advances the rest of the mint and is repaid in PT within the
same transaction. The mechanism is described in full under
[Flash swaps](/protocol/flash-swaps).

## Slippage

Every user-facing swap takes an explicit bound and reverts if it is not met:

| Operation | Bound |
|-----------|-------|
| Buying PT / YT | `max_v_in`, `v_in_max` | TODO: im realizing this naming is asymetrical
| Selling PT / YT | `min_v_out` |
| Expired exits | `min_shares_out` |

For [zaps](/using-ybc/zaps) the bound is expressed once, in base-asset terms
(`min_asset_out` / `max_asset_in`), and covers pool price and vault rate together.

## Providing liquidity

```
Router::deposit   →  AMM::deposit
Router::withdraw  →  AMM::withdraw
```

An LP position is a share of the pool's `(PT, V)` reserves; shares are tracked
per user by the AMM. LPs earn the trading fee on both legs, minus the protocol's
reserve cut.

A few properties worth knowing before supplying:

- **Reserves are not balance-read.** Tokens sent directly to the
  pool never enter pricing or share math, so donation attacks do nothing.
- **`MINIMUM_LIQUIDITY` (100 shares) is burned on the first deposit**, and that
  first deposit must exceed it. This is how first-depositor share inflation is prevented.
- **The protocol's cut leaves at trade time**, not out of your position. By the
  time you withdraw there is nothing left to deduct.
- **The fee configuration is immutable.** Treasury address and reserve fee rate
  are written once by the pool constructor and have no setter. They can be read at entry knowing they can never change in a live market.

To add liquidity starting from the base asset, `zap_asset_for_lp` takes a
caller-supplied `pt_to_buy`. The pool only accepts its two legs in the current
reserve ratio, so the frontend simulates for that figure; the router refunds
whatever the pool declines, meaning a slightly wrong number costs a refund rather
than a failed transaction.
