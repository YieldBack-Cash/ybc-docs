At maturity the market's [exchange rate](/concepts/exchange-rate) locks and
positions freeze in **asset value**. Nothing expires, nothing is liquidated, and
there is no deadline to exit — but no new yield accrues to YT either.

## Redeeming PT

```
YieldManager::redeem_principal(from, pt_amount)
```

Available **after maturity only**. Burns PT and returns vault shares at
`max(live_vault_rate, locked_rate)`. The rate divides, so flooring it caps the
shares paid out:

- **If the vault kept growing**, you receive fewer shares worth more — exactly
  face value. The freed difference stays in the yield manager as surplus, swept
  to the treasury by a permissionless `collect_surplus`. That counter only ever
  holds shares no user has a claim on, so redemption stays open and solvent
  indefinitely.
- **If the vault fell**, the locked rate floors the calculation, so you receive
  the share count reserved at maturity and no more. Those shares are worth less
  than face value and you bear that loss — the floor exists so a redemption
  cannot draw more shares than its PT was backed by.

## Claiming YT yield

`YieldToken::claim_yield` pays out the yield accrued to your YT and burns dust YT
past maturity.

<Callout type="warn">
**Late claims pay locked value, not appreciated shares.** After the rate locks, a
claim pays the number of shares that its locked-rate *asset value* buys at the
*live* rate. If the vault kept growing, that is fewer shares than the frozen
accrual.

Two claims of the same accrual made at different times receive different share
counts — but the same asset value. This is the intended "no new yield after
maturity" semantics.
</Callout>

## Exiting everything at once

```
Router::exit_expired(vault, maturity, to, lp_shares, min_shares_out)
```

A one-call unwind of an expired position. It withdraws your LP position, redeems
your **entire** PT balance, sweeps YT-accrued yield via `claim_yield`, and bounds
the total vault shares delivered by `min_shares_out`.

`exit_expired_to_asset` does the same and redeems the proceeds through the vault
so you end up holding the base asset. Note its `sweep_allowance` behaviour — see
[Zaps](/using-ybc/zaps).
