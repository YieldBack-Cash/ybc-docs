Every core operation is denominated in **V** (vault shares). Zaps let you arrive
and leave holding only the vault's underlying asset and never touch shares at all. Each one wraps an existing operation in a vault deposit or
redeem.

## The zaps

| Zap | Path |
|-----|------|
| `zap_asset_for_pt` / `zap_pt_for_asset` | asset ↔ V ↔ PT (AMM spot) |
| `zap_asset_for_yt` / `zap_yt_for_asset` | asset ↔ V ↔ YT (flash swap) |
| `zap_asset_for_split` / `zap_split_for_asset` | asset ↔ V ↔ PT + YT (mint/burn) |
| `zap_asset_for_lp` / `zap_lp_for_asset` | asset ↔ V ↔ LP position |
| `exit_expired_to_asset` | expired LP + PT + YT → asset |

## Four properties hold across all of them

<Steps>
<Step>
**The router still custodies nothing.** Every leg names *you* as the
token-holding party — the vault deposit passes your address as `from`, `receiver`
and `operator` alike. A revert anywhere unwinds the whole chain and strands
nothing.
</Step>
<Step>
**Slippage is bounded once, in base-asset terms.** A single `min_asset_out` or
`max_asset_in` covers pool price *and* vault rate together.
</Step>
<Step>
**The vault is called only through SEP-56**. `query_asset`, `convert_to_assets`,
`deposit` and `redeem`. Nothing vault-specific, so any compliant vault can back a
market. The asset address is read live from `query_asset`,
**once per invocation** so there is nothing that can go
stale.
</Step>
</Steps>

## The `exit_expired_to_asset` allowance

<Callout type="warn">
`exit_expired_to_asset` converts your **entire** vault-share balance up to
`sweep_allowance`. This includes shares held for unrelated markets. **Size the
allowance to the expected proceeds**, not higher.
</Callout>

The reason is a transaction-budget trade-off. The yield manager authenticates the
caller, so the amount it takes has to be a ceiling signed in advance rather than
a delta measured mid-call. In exchange, it gathers loose shares with one cheap
`transfer_from` and redeems the combined total in a **single** vault redemption
instead of two, which is what brings the call inside Soroban's resource limits
when an LP position is involved.

## Transaction cost

A zap's cost is dominated by how many times it makes the vault do real work.
Against a lending vault like Blend, a deposit or redeem is a pool submission (reserve updates, position updates, emissions accounting) and even a rate *read*
accrues interest and reads pool state.

The governing principle is that **the rate is loaded once per invocation and
threaded downward**, never re-fetched by each layer. The pool loads it once and
hands it to the flash callbacks; the yield manager passes it on to the yield
token. The underlying vault is therefore read at most once per transaction (and
past maturity not at all) since the yield manager's rate is locked and answers
from its own storage.

See [Vault integration](/protocol/vault-integration) for where that rate comes
from and why the pool asks the yield manager rather than the vault.
